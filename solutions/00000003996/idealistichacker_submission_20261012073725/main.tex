\begin{abstract}
The proposed diagonal $n=k^2$ does not supply contradictory Ramsey CNFs, even eventually.
For every threshold there is a larger $k$ and a real two-coloring of the complete graph on
$k^2$ vertices with no monochromatic $k$-clique. The standard Ramsey CNF is therefore
satisfiable and admits no resolution refutation, so the claimed matching finite upper bound
cannot hold. Our unbounded family uses lexicographic powers of the five-cycle and an actual
finite injection. The pinned Lean project proves both color clique bounds, the cardinality
and restriction bridges, satisfaction of every standard CNF clause, general resolution
soundness and a closed negation of eventual refutability. A single small counterexample is
not used as a substitute for the asymptotic argument.
\end{abstract}

\section{The exact claim and the clause being refuted}
Both original languages describe a formalized contradictory Ramsey principle, a resolution
length lower bound $2^{\Omega(k)}$, and the selection $n=k^2$ making upper and lower exponent
constants match. We use the ordinary two-edge-color Ramsey counterexample CNF: it forbids
every all-red and every all-blue $k$-vertex subset of the complete graph on $n$ vertices.
A satisfying assignment is precisely a coloring with no monochromatic $k$-clique.

Any finite asymptotic resolution upper bound for this selected diagonal necessarily entails
\begin{equation}\label{eq:necessary}
 \exists K\;\forall k\ge K:\quad \mathrm{RAM}(k^2,k)
 \text{ has a finite resolution refutation}.
\end{equation}
We prove the negation of this necessary clause. We do \emph{not} claim the lower bound alone
is false: a satisfiable formula has no refutation, so a lower-bound-only convention assigning
infinite minimal length would not produce a contradiction. It is the asserted contradictory
status and finite matching upper bound that fail. Conditioning the diagonal on $n\ge R(k,k)$,
or selecting only an unspecified unsatisfiable subsequence, would be a different statement.
The unchanged exact bilingual conjecture and its Git/SHA-256 identities are included.

\section{Actual recursive graphs and both clique bounds}
Let $C_5$ be the cycle on digits $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$, with adjacent consecutive digits modulo five.
Both $C_5$ and its complement have clique size at most two; the formal project checks this
for all actual finite digit subsets, not by assigning a clique number.

Define $W_0$ to contain the empty word, and $W_{t+1}=\{0,1,2,3,4\}\times W_t$.
Then $|W_t|=5^t$. The red graph $G_t$ is the following real simple graph on $W_t$:
for different words, inspect the first digit where they differ; the edge is red exactly
when those two digits are adjacent in $C_5$. Equivalently, at unequal first digits use
$C_5$ adjacency, and inside an equal-first-digit block use the suffix graph $G_{t-1}$.
The blue graph is the actual complement, not an unrelated graph with an assigned bound.

We prove by induction that every red or blue clique has at most $2^t$ vertices.
At $t=0$ the only vertex makes the bound $1$ immediate. At the successor step, the first
digits occupied by a monochromatic clique form a clique in $C_5$ or its complement, hence
occupy at most two blocks. Within each block, suffixes form a clique of the same color and
have size at most $2^t$ by induction. The image/fiber cardinality sum therefore gives
$2\cdot2^t=2^{t+1}$. Lean proves this generic block lemma and the induction for
\emph{every actual clique}, rather than enumerating finitely many word lengths.

\section{The genuine diagonal restriction and unbounded parameters}
For $t\ge2$ put $k_t=2^t+1$ and $n_t=k_t^2$. There are enough words:
\[
 (2^t+1)^2\le5^t.
\]
The base case is $25=25$. In the induction step, writing $a=2^t$, the exact nonnegative
difference is
\[
 5(a+1)^2-(2a+1)^2=a^2+6a+4\ge0.
\]
Thus the successor inequality follows from the preceding one and $5^{t+1}=5\cdot5^t$.

Choose an actual injection from $\mathrm{Fin}(n_t)$ into $W_t$ and pull back $G_t$.
The formal choice uses the proved finite cardinality comparison and standard
\texttt{Classical.choice}; no prescribed lexicographic initial segment is assumed or
claimed as a computed output. Clique cardinalities cannot increase under an injective
pullback, and complement commutes with that pullback. Hence this genuine graph on exactly
$k_t^2$ vertices has no red or blue $k_t$-clique, since $2^t<k_t$.

The parameters $k_t$ are unbounded. In particular, for every natural threshold $K$ the
project proves existence of $k\ge K$ and an actual graph on $\mathrm{Fin}(k^2)$ whose red
graph and actual complement are both $k$-clique-free. This covers the eventual/asymptotic
quantifier, not merely a finite-prefix failure.

\section{Standard CNF and ordinary resolution are formally connected}
The edge variables are the actual unordered edges of the complete graph, excluding loops.
For every $k$-subset $S$, the CNF contains the two clauses
\[
 \bigvee_{e\in\binom S2}\neg x_e,\qquad
 \bigvee_{e\in\binom S2}x_e.
\]
They forbid respectively the all-red and all-blue coloring. For a real graph $g$, assign
$x_e$ true exactly when its edge lies in $g$. If $g$ and $g^c$ are both $k$-clique-free,
every $k$-subset has an edge of each color. The project proves that this actual assignment
satisfies \emph{every generated clause}, with no omitted subsets, arbitrary named variables
or unused forbidden patterns. Repeated clauses may be deduplicated without changing truth.

Clauses are finite signed-literal sets. Ordinary resolution removes opposite pivot literals
from the two earlier parent clauses and unions the remaining literals; weakening enlarges
an earlier clause. The proof relation has only input, resolution and weakening constructors.
For every assignment, satisfaction of the parents entails satisfaction of the resolvent.
Structural induction proves soundness of any such derivation over any finite vertex set.
A satisfying assignment therefore excludes derivation of the empty clause, with no bound
on proof length. Finite DAG or listed proofs unroll to these trees without changing the
existence of a finite proof; this standard mathematical correspondence is explained in
prose, not advertised as an additional general Lean DAG theorem.

Applying soundness to the graphs from the previous section proves
\[
 \forall K\;\exists k\ge K:\quad
 \mathrm{RAM}(k^2,k)\text{ is satisfiable and has no resolution refutation}.
\]
\begin{theorem}
Conjecture 00000003996's finite matching-upper-bound claim for the selected diagonal
$n=k^2$ is false under the standard Ramsey CNF and ordinary resolution conventions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An eventual finite upper bound implies~\eqref{eq:necessary}. Given its threshold $K$, the
actual coloring family supplies $k\ge K$ with a satisfying assignment. Soundness excludes
the alleged resolution refutation. This contradicts~\eqref{eq:necessary}.
\end{proof}
