\begin{abstract}
For a finite straight-edge graph in the real plane, suppose that every two
distinct edges have exactly one common point. We prove that the number of
edges is at most the number of vertices. The argument permits collinear
vertices, intersections at endpoints, and nonincident vertices lying on
edges. A complete Lean 4 proof accompanies the report.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and conventions}
The supplied English statement defines a geometric thrackle as a
``straight-edge graph in the plane in which every two edges intersect
exactly once,'' and asks for the bound $|E|\leq |V|$. Both original language
versions are preserved verbatim in \texttt{source/original.md}.

We use the ordinary graph convention: a finite undirected simple graph
$G=(V,E)$, with distinct vertices represented by distinct points of
$\mathbb R^2$. For $u,v\in V$, write their positions simply as $u,v$, and
draw an edge $uv$ as the closed segment
\[
 \seg uv=\{(1-t)u+tv:0\leq t\leq 1\}.
\]
An unordered edge is counted once. An isolated vertex is allowed.
The hypothesis is that $\seg uv\cap\seg xy$ is a singleton for any two
distinct edges $uv,xy$. In particular, incident edges meet only at their
shared endpoint. We impose no general-position assumption and do not
require a nonincident edge pair to cross in their relative interiors.
Thus the proof also covers the more restrictive usual drawing convention
that forbids a nonincident vertex from lying inside an edge. Parallel
copies of a nondegenerate straight edge would have a whole segment in
common and therefore cannot satisfy the hypothesis.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Under these hypotheses, $|E|\leq |V|$.
\end{theorem}

The reference to the numerical coefficient $1.393$ in the supplied
statement is background to the requested improvement. Our result is the
stated stronger bound $|E|\leq |V|$; no historical or priority assertion
about the bounds is needed in the proof.

\section{A geometric sign lemma}
For vectors $r=(r_1,r_2)$ and $s=(s_1,s_2)$ define
$\detp rs=r_1s_2-r_2s_1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sign}
Let $v,a,b,x\in\mathbb R^2$, with $a\ne v$ and $b\ne v$. Suppose that
\[
 \seg va\cap\seg vb=\{v\},\qquad
 \seg va\cap\seg ax=\{a\},\qquad
 \seg vb\cap\seg ax\ne\varnothing.
\]
Then
\[
 \detp{a-v}{b-v}\,\detp{a-v}{x-v}>0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $p\in\seg vb\cap\seg ax$. If $p=v$, then $v$ belongs to
$\seg va\cap\seg ax=\{a\}$, contrary to $a\ne v$. If $p=a$, then $a$
belongs to $\seg va\cap\seg vb=\{v\}$, again a contradiction.

We claim $D=\detp{a-v}{p-v}\ne0$. Otherwise $v,a,p$ are collinear.
Among three collinear points, one is between the other two, including the
endpoints in the meaning of ``between.'' All cases give a contradiction:
\begin{itemize}
\item If $a\in\seg vp$, then $\seg vp\subseteq\seg vb$, so
      $a\in\seg va\cap\seg vb=\{v\}$.
\item If $p\in\seg va$, then $p\in\seg va\cap\seg vb=\{v\}$.
\item If $v\in\seg ap$, then $\seg ap\subseteq\seg ax$, so
      $v\in\seg va\cap\seg ax=\{a\}$.
\end{itemize}
These contradict respectively $a\ne v$, $p\ne v$, and $a\ne v$.
The subset assertions follow from the convexity of a segment.

Write $p=(1-t)v+tb=(1-s)a+sx$, where $s,t\in[0,1]$. As $p\ne v,a$,
we have $t>0$ and $s>0$. Bilinearity and alternation of the determinant give
\[
 D=t\detp{a-v}{b-v}=s\detp{a-v}{x-v}.
\]
Hence
\[
 0<D^2=st\detp{a-v}{b-v}\detp{a-v}{x-v}.
\]
Since $st>0$, the claimed strict inequality follows. This argument deals
explicitly with possible collinearity rather than assuming it away.
\end{proof}

\section{At most two neighbors of degree other than one}
Write $d(v)$ for the degree of a vertex. A leaf is a vertex of degree one.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:two}
Every vertex has at most two neighbors that are not leaves.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $a$ be a neighbor of $v$ that is not a leaf. Since it already has the
neighbor $v$, its degree is at least two. Choose a neighbor $x\ne v$ of
$a$. For any other neighbor $b\ne a$ of $v$, the edges $va,vb,ax$ are
pairwise distinct: equality of $vb$ and $ax$ would force either $v=a$ or
$v=x$, both excluded. Their singleton intersections satisfy
Lemma~\ref{lem:sign}. Consequently,
\[
 \detp{a-v}{b-v}\,\detp{a-v}{x-v}>0
 \quad\text{for every such }b.
\]
In particular all the determinants $\detp{a-v}{b-v}$, as $b$ ranges over
the other neighbors of $v$, have the same strict sign. The same conclusion
holds for each nonleaf neighbor in place of $a$.

Suppose three distinct nonleaf neighbors $a,b,c$ exist, and put
\[
 A=\detp{a-v}{b-v},\qquad
 B=\detp{a-v}{c-v},\qquad
 C=\detp{b-v}{c-v}.
\]
The conclusion at $a$ gives $AB>0$. At $b$, determinant antisymmetry
gives $(-A)C>0$. At $c$ it gives $(-B)(-C)=BC>0$. The first and third
inequalities say that $A,B,C$ have the same strict sign, whereas the
second says that $A,C$ have opposite signs. This contradiction proves the
lemma.
\end{proof}

\section{Counting the edges}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Let $L$ be the set of leaves, let $\ell(v)$ be the number of leaf
neighbors of $v$, and let $\mathbf1_L(v)$ be one when $v\in L$ and zero
otherwise. Lemma~\ref{lem:two} gives
$d(v)\leq2+\ell(v)$ for every vertex. If $v$ itself is a leaf then
$d(v)+\mathbf1_L(v)=2\leq2+\ell(v)$; otherwise the indicator is zero.
Thus, in all cases,
\[
 d(v)+\mathbf1_L(v)\leq2+\ell(v).
\]
Summing over vertices and reversing the order in the finite adjacency
sum gives
\[
 \sum_{v\in V}\ell(v)
 =\sum_{w\in L}\#\{v\in V:vw\in E\}
 =\sum_{w\in L}d(w)=|L|.
\]
The degree-sum formula therefore yields
\[
 2|E|+|L|\leq2|V|+|L|.
\]
Canceling $|L|$ and dividing by two proves $|E|\leq|V|$. This includes
the empty graph, isolated vertices, disconnected graphs, and edges whose
two endpoints are both leaves.
\end{proof}

\section{Formal statement and verification}
The project uses Lean \texttt{4.19.0} and stock Mathlib at revision
\texttt{c44e0c8ee63ca166450922a373c7409c5d26b00b}. The committed Lake
manifest pins the complete dependency set.

The structure \texttt{GeometricThrackle.Drawing G} contains an injective
position map into $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$, and an existence-and-uniqueness
assertion for the intersection of each pair of distinct unordered edges.
The custom segment definition is proved equal to Mathlib's real closed
segment in \texttt{seg\_eq\_segment}. The final declaration is
\begin{quote}\ttfamily\small
GeometricThrackle.geometric\_thrackle\_bound
\end{quote}
and its conclusion is
\begin{quote}\ttfamily\small
G.edgeFinset.card $\leq$ Fintype.card V.
\end{quote}
The finite vertex type and simple graph are arbitrary. The decidable
adjacency instance appearing in the Lean statement provides finite
enumeration; it is available classically for every such graph and adds
no mathematical restriction.

The source files correspond directly to the preceding proof:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{Geometry.lean}: the segment definition, collinearity step,
      and Lemma~\ref{lem:sign} as \texttt{det\_strict\_side}.
\item \texttt{Angular.lean}: the sign contradiction and the cardinal
      bound on nonleaf neighbors.
\item \texttt{Counting.lean}: the finite-sum cancellation proving the
      edge bound from the local neighbor bound.
\item \texttt{Main.lean}: the exact drawing hypotheses, their application
      to the geometric lemma, and the final theorem.
\end{itemize}
The verification script rebuilds all four local modules, checks the
pinned manifest and toolchain, scans the authored Lean sources for
incomplete or unsafe proof mechanisms, and checks the printed axioms of
the final theorem. The printed dependencies are precisely
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}, the
standard foundational axioms used by Mathlib. There are no added axioms,
admitted goals, or computation-based trust extensions. No auxiliary
numerical computation is required by the proof.

The proof was derived from the supplied statement and repository rules,
with access to stock Mathlib and the pinned toolchain. No prior
candidate-specific solution or online mathematical proof was consulted.
The local provenance notes record the independent geometric assessment
and the division of formalization work. This report makes no claim of
historical novelty.
