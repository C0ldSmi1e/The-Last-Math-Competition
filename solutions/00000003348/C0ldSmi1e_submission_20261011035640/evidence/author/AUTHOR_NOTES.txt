Interpretation and scope:
The brief bilingual statement does not give an ambient category, Julia-set
construction, definition of pressure, potential, or dimension convention. We
use complex polynomials of degree at least two, a standard subclass of maps
with Julia sets. The filled Julia set is defined by bounded forward orbits;
the Julia set is its boundary. The Julia locus is where uniform exponential
expansion is asserted, as in standard holomorphic dynamics. We do not claim
that complex squaring is expanding on the entire complex plane. Cantor type
means the entire Julia subspace is homeomorphic to the standard Cantor space
N -> Bool. Merely containing an embedded Cantor subset would be a different
claim and is not refuted. Likewise any additional unstated restriction forcing
a disconnected repeller would be a different conjecture.

The polynomial is X^2. The formalization computes actual evaluation and
iteration. The orbit-bound characterization of the filled Julia set is proved
in both directions. The Julia identity follows from the topological frontier
of the closed unit disk. Every complex and real Frechet tangent derivative of
the nth iterate has norm 2^n times the vector norm on this set. The set is
compact and invariant, and every iterate is genuinely complex differentiable.
A supplementary local distance estimate has radius 1/2 and expansion factor
3/2; it is not a global distance estimate. Connectedness and distinct points
1, -1 disprove total disconnectedness. An actual homeomorphism-to-Cantor bridge
establishes that the Julia set is not of Cantor type.

No pressure or dimension is defined or computed. The conjecture asserts both
a Cantor-type conclusion and a dimension-pressure conclusion. Failure of the
necessary first clause refutes that conjunction regardless of the second
clause. The generic additionalConclusion theorem only expresses this elementary
logical fact; its arbitrary predicate does not purport to formalize pressure.
The normal-family definition of Julia sets and its equivalence to the boundary
definition for degree-at-least-two polynomials is used as a standard definition
convention supported by the reference below, not newly formalized in Lean.
