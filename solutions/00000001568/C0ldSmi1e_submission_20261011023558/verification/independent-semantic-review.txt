Both languages define a geometric thrackle by straight edges in the plane, with every pair of edges intersecting exactly once, and assert that its edge count is at most its vertex count. The geometric quantifiers agree. The statement requires the bound for every such graph/drawing, not merely a selected configuration.

1. The positive-parameter and determinant step

Let v-a and v-b be distinct edges, with a != b, and let a-x be a second edge at a with x != v. Write positions relative to v as A, B and X. Let p be the intersection of segments [a,x] and [v,b]. The relevant edges really are distinct: in a simple undirected graph, a-x cannot equal v-a because x != v and cannot equal v-b because a differs from v and b.

The pairwise singleton conditions give [v,a] intersection [a,x] = {a} and [v,a] intersection [v,b] = {v}, since their indicated endpoints already belong to both segments. Therefore p != v and p != a. In closed-segment coordinates,

  p-v = t B = (1-s) A + s X,   0 <= s,t <= 1,

these exclusions give s > 0 and t > 0. Values s=1 or t=1 are allowed and do not weaken the argument. Bilinearity gives

  t det(A,B) = s det(A,X).

This yields identical signs only after excluding zero determinants; a nonnegative relation alone is insufficient for the later strict-side contradiction.

2. Independent check of the collinear exclusion

Suppose v,a,b were collinear. Vertex injectivity gives A != 0 and B != 0. The singleton intersection [v,a] intersection [v,b] = {v} forces a and b to lie on opposite rays from v: two nonzero segments on the same ray share a nontrivial initial segment.

Because t > 0, p lies strictly on the b ray. The segment from a to p therefore contains v. As p belongs to [a,x], that segment is a subsegment of [a,x], so v belongs to [a,x]. But v also belongs to [v,a], whereas those two incident edges have unique intersection a and v != a. Contradiction.

Thus det(A,B) != 0. The displayed identity and s,t > 0 also give det(A,X) != 0, and both determinants have the same strict sign. This is a consequence of actual segment intersections, not an assumption that all triples of vertices are noncollinear.

3. Three nonleaf neighbors are impossible

For a nonleaf neighbor a of v, fix a second neighbor x != v once. For every other neighbor b of v, the previous step gives det(a-v,b-v) the same strict sign as det(a-v,x-v). Consequently, any two other neighbors lie on the same strict side of the directed line v-a.

If v has three distinct nonleaf neighbors a,b,c, set d_ab=det(a-v,b-v), d_ac=det(a-v,c-v), d_bc=det(b-v,c-v). At a, d_ab and d_ac have the same nonzero sign. At b, -d_ab and d_bc have the same nonzero sign. Thus d_bc has the sign opposite to d_ac. At c, however, -d_ac and -d_bc must have the same sign. This contradicts the previous conclusion. Each vertex therefore has at most two neighbors whose degree is at least two.

The choice of x need not be distinct from b or c. In particular, x=b gives a triangle situation and p=b, with s=t=1; the same proof works. No argument should silently strengthen the hypotheses to four distinct vertices or require intersections to lie in both open segments.

4. Counting step

Let l(v) be the number of degree-one neighbors of v. Every neighbor has positive degree. Thus for degree(v)>=2, its neighbors partition into degree-one neighbors and neighbors of degree at least two, giving degree(v)<=l(v)+2. If degree(v)=1, then degree(v)+1<=2+l(v) trivially. If degree(v)=0, the inequality is also immediate. Hence for all vertices,

  degree(v) + indicator(degree(v)=1) <= 2 + l(v).

The sum of l(v) is exactly the number of degree-one vertices, because each leaf occurs in the neighbor set of exactly one vertex. This remains true for an isolated single-edge component, whose two endpoints are both leaves. Summing cancels the two leaf-count terms. The handshake identity then gives 2|E|<=2|V| and therefore |E|<=|V|. The empty graph, isolated vertices and disconnected graphs require no additional hypothesis. Neither parity of cycles nor connectedness is used.

5. Drawing and semantic obligations

- Vertices must be embedded injectively, and an edge must join two different vertices. These are ordinary straight-line graph-drawing conventions, but they must be stated. They do not follow solely from a singleton-intersection predicate if zero-length edges or coincident named vertices are allowed.
- In particular, arbitrary zero-length loops at one point could evade such a predicate; this is not a standard nondegenerate straight-edge drawing. A SimpleGraph excludes loops by definition. Parallel nondegenerate edges with the same endpoints would have an entire segment in common and are already incompatible with the stated pairwise condition.
- "Every two edges" must mean distinct unordered edges. Treating opposite orientations as different edges would wrongly demand that an edge intersect itself at only one point.
- The intersection property must assert existence and uniqueness of a common point of actual closed segments. It must not merely choose one intersection or count only proper interior crossings.
- Adjacent edges meet at their shared endpoint, which counts as the required intersection. The proof must retain this. Nonincident endpoint-on-segment contacts, a vertex lying on another edge, or several nonincident edges passing through one point need not be prohibited by this reduction; allowing them makes the statement more general.
- No blanket no-three-collinear, proper-crossing, convex-position, minimum-degree, connectedness, nonempty-graph or vertex-number lower-bound assumption is justified by the source or needed by the argument.
- Finiteness is the natural scope of the numerical n-vertex edge bound. The theorem should quantify over every finite simple graph and every drawing satisfying the genuine segment condition.

6. Full conclusion and numerical trailing phrase

The proof establishes the source's asserted upper bound on every drawing. It need not prove equality of the maximum with n for every n, or construct extremizers, because the conjecture asks for "at most" n. The trailing Lovasz-Pach-Szegedy sentence states the intended improvement to n; no external historical attribution or claimed sharpness has been verified in this source-only review.

If the report makes the comparison numerically, n <= floor(1.393 n) for natural n. It must not claim a pointwise strict improvement for every n: the two bounds coincide at n=0,1,2. The proof should not silently replace the precise n bound with an asymptotic claim or with a statement for sufficiently large n.

B. Geometry.lean: strictness and every degeneracy

The actual strict-side proof is sound and differs harmlessly from the independently checked ray argument. It first excludes p=v using the intersection [v,a] intersect [a,x]={a}, and excludes p=a using [v,a] intersect [v,b]={v}. Both contradictions require only a!=v.

It then assumes det(a-v,p-v)=0, explicitly derives real collinearity of {v,a,p} by coordinates, and uses all three weak-betweenness possibilities. If a lies between v and p, convexity of [v,b] puts a in [v,b], contradicting the first incident singleton. If p lies between a and v, p lies in [v,a], contradicting p!=v and the singleton at v. If v lies between p and a, convexity of [a,x] puts v in [a,x], contradicting the singleton at a. This exhaustive argument includes endpoint equality in the weak betweenness alternatives; the proved exclusions remove the equalities. It does not assume general position. The additional b!=v argument is unused, which is harmless because the remaining hypotheses already exclude the relevant degeneracy.

After extracting segment parameters, the proof derives t>0 from p!=v and s>0 from p!=a. It retains t<=1 and s<=1; neither parameter is assumed strictly below one. Direct determinant expansion gives det(a-v,p-v)=t*det(a-v,b-v)=s*det(a-v,x-v). The common value is nonzero, so its square is positive. Division of the resulting product identity by the positive factor t*s is logically represented using positivity of multiplication. The final product is strictly positive. No sign or zero case is lost.

The coordinate collinearity lemma handles both the first coordinate zero case (where a!=v forces the second nonzero) and the first coordinate nonzero case. Its divisions therefore have explicit nonzero denominators. Its conclusion is genuine collinearity, not a renamed determinant hypothesis.

C. Main.lean: graph-to-geometry bridge

D. Angular.lean: at most two nonleaf neighbors

The abstract angular lemma quantifies over an arbitrary real-valued skew function D and the strict-side property. The resulting three strict sign comparisons are inconsistent, as shown by exhaustive positive-product cases and linear inequalities.
